**CHAPTER IV --- THE MEDICAL FACTS: AN ACUTE ILLNESS AND A MEDICALLY
UNAVOIDABLE SURGERY**

6\. In June 2025 --- more than seven months into the policy --- the
Complainant developed **acute obstruction to the passage of urine**. The
Discharge Summary of RS Maitri Health Care (Annexure C-4) records,
verbatim:

> **Diagnosis:** \"TIGHT PHIMOSIS WITH BALANITIS WITH MEATAL STENOSIS\"
>
> **Brief history of presenting illness:** \"POOR FLOW OF URINE\"
>
> **Procedure:** \"CIRCUMCISION + MEATOPLASTY DONE UNDER SA ON
> 20/06/2025\"
>
> **Course during hospitalization:** \"IV FLUIDS AND IV ANTIBIOTICS\"

7\. These four entries of the hospital\'s own contemporaneous record
establish, without any need for argument: (i) acute urinary obstruction;
(ii) **active infection** --- balanitis with urinary tract infection
(UTI), treated with intravenous antibiotics, as also certified by the
treating Urologist (Annexure C-12); (iii) surgery performed **on the
very day of admission**, which is possible only where surgery is
medically unavoidable; and (iv) treatment by a qualified surgical team
under anaesthesia in a hospital setting.

8\. The excised preputial tissue was sent for histopathological
examination --- a step which was **clinically indicated**, because the
Histopathology Report of Max Lab dated 30.06.2025, No. V-6630/25
(Annexure C-9), records in its Gross Description, verbatim:

> *\"Externally shows cauliflower like lesion measures 3.5 x 3 x 0.8
> cm.\"*

A \"cauliflower-like\" growth is a morphology that any competent surgeon
is duty-bound to have examined for malignancy.

9\. The Report\'s Impression reads, verbatim:

> **\"Consistent with Lichen sclerosus (balanitis xerotica
> obliterans).\"**

The biopsy thus **confirmed BXO (Balanitis Xerotica Obliterans / Lichen
Sclerosus)** --- a recognised premalignant condition of the penis, as
certified by the treating specialist (Chapter V below) and as recorded
in standard medical literature (Annexure C-13).

10\. The medical sequence is therefore complete and entirely
documentary: acute urinary obstruction → recorded poor urinary flow →
active infection requiring IV antibiotics → medically unavoidable urgent
surgery → clinically indicated biopsy → biopsy-confirmed BXO. Not one
link in this chain rests on the Complainant\'s say-so; every link is a
hospital or laboratory record.

**10-A. Plain meaning of the medical terms (for ready reference of this
Hon'ble Commission).** The conditions recorded in the Complainant's own
hospital and laboratory documents are, in ordinary language, the
following: (a) **Phimosis** is an abnormal tightening of the foreskin
which prevents its normal retraction and, in its severe form, obstructs
the free passage of urine; (b) **Balanitis** is an active inflammation
and infection of the glans and foreskin; (c) **Meatal stenosis** is a
narrowing of the urinary opening which further obstructs the flow of
urine; and (d) **Balanitis Xerotica Obliterans (BXO)**, also called
genital **Lichen Sclerosus**, is a chronic scarring disease of the
foreskin that is medically recognised as a **pre-malignant
(pre-cancerous)** condition --- that is, a condition carrying a known
risk of progressing to cancer of the penis if it is not treated. None of
these is a cosmetic complaint; each is an organic disease recorded
contemporaneously by the treating hospital, and together they explain
why urgent surgery was undertaken on the very day of admission.

**CHAPTER VII-A --- THE REPUDIATION FAILED THE DUTY TO PASS A REASONED,
SPEAKING DECISION**

19-A. It is a settled incident of fair dealing, and of the insurer's
obligation of utmost good faith, that the repudiation of a health claim
must be a reasoned, "speaking" decision. Such a decision must (i)
identify the specific policy condition relied upon; (ii) apply that
condition to the specific medical facts of the case; and (iii) deal with
the material evidence placed on record by the insured. A rejection that
merely reproduces the text of a clause --- without naming the listed
illness said to attract it, and without engaging with the biopsy, the
treating specialist's certificate, or the exception written into the
very same clause --- is not an application of mind; it is a template.

19-B. The record discloses precisely such a template. The rejection
e-mail dated 25.06.2025, the repudiation dated 26.06.2025, and the
Grievance Redressal Officer's reply dated 15.07.2026 are, in substance,
identical: each recites the general "Specific Illnesses" waiting period;
none identifies any listed condition in the Schedule; none refers to the
Histopathology Report; none refers to the treating super-specialist's
certificate; and none refers to the Ombudsman's findings in paras 13--14
of the Award. A process that repeats the same unreasoned conclusion at
three successive stages, in the teeth of the medical record, is itself a
distinct and independent deficiency in service, quite apart from the
ultimate merits of the claim.

**CHAPTER IX --- THE POLICY CLAUSE, READ LAST AND READ FAIRLY, SUPPORTS
THE CLAIM**

23\. The Policy Schedule (Annexure C-1) prescribes for the Complainant,
verbatim: *\"Specific illness --- 2 years waiting period on specific
illness --- NOTE: Refer Annexure for the complete disease list\"*. The
only entry of that Annexure relied upon by the Opposite Party reads,
verbatim:

> **\"Any Surgery of the genitourinary system \*unless necessitated by
> malignancy\*.\"**

24\. The clause is therefore **not an absolute exclusion**: the insurer
itself carved out genitourinary surgery connected with malignancy from
the waiting period. On the admitted and documented facts --- a grossly
suspicious \"cauliflower like lesion\", a biopsy-confirmed premalignant
disease, and the treating uro-oncologist\'s certification that BXO is
*\"a pre-malignant condition\"* carrying, if untreated, the *\"risk of
penile cancer\"* --- the present surgery was **\"necessitated by
malignancy\"** within the true object and purpose of that exception.

25\. It is significant what the policy **nowhere** says. The policy:

• nowhere **defines** \"necessitated by malignancy\";

• nowhere **excludes premalignant conditions** from the exception;

• nowhere requires an **existing or established cancer**;

• nowhere requires **histologically proven invasive carcinoma**; and

• nowhere states that **preventive oncological surgery** is excluded.

The insurer\'s reading --- that the exception operates only after cancer
has fully established itself --- is therefore not the only possible
interpretation, and it is an interpretation that defeats the evident
object of the exception: that genuinely oncological genitourinary
surgery must not be made to wait out two years, because in such cases
delay is medically dangerous. A construction that pays for surgery only
after the cancer arrives, but refuses the very surgery that prevents it,
frustrates both the clause and the purpose of health insurance.

26\. To be precise: the Complainant does **not** contend that he had
established cancer. The contention --- certified by the treating
uro-oncologist and supported by standard medical literature --- is that
**BXO is a medically recognised premalignant condition carrying a known
risk of progression to penile squamous cell carcinoma**, and that the
surgery was undertaken because of that documented malignant potential,
not for any cosmetic or elective reason.

27\. **In the alternative, and only if necessary:** if this Hon\'ble
Commission finds the expression \"necessitated by malignancy\" to be
genuinely capable of two reasonable meanings --- (i) surgery for
established malignancy, or (ii) surgery necessitated by a disease
process of malignant potential --- then, by the settled rule of
construction of insurance contracts (contra proferentem), the ambiguity
in a clause drafted solely by the insurer must be resolved **in favour
of the insured**. This submission is made strictly in the alternative;
the Complainant\'s primary case rests on the medical evidence itself.

**CHAPTER X --- DEFICIENCY IN SERVICE AND UNFAIR TRADE PRACTICE**

28\. Repudiating a genuine, medically necessary, fully documented claim
--- without examining the biopsy, without obtaining any medical opinion,
without applying the exception in the insurer\'s own clause, and without
disclosing even the sub-clause relied upon --- constitutes **deficiency
in service** within Section 2(11) and **unfair trade practice** within
Section 2(47) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. The arbitrary
repudiation has caused the Complainant financial loss, prolonged
harassment across three fora over more than a year, and mental agony.

**28-A. Particulars of the deficiency.** The deficiency in service
consists not of a single act but of a continuing course of conduct. In
particular, the Opposite Party: (a) repudiated the claim without ever
examining the biopsy report; (b) obtained no medical opinion of its own
at any stage; (c) failed to apply the express exception "unless
necessitated by malignancy" contained in the very clause it invoked; (d)
pronounced, on 25.06.2025, a medical conclusion ("not related to a
malignancy") five days before the Histopathology Report of 30.06.2025
even existed; (e) failed to disclose, even before the Ombudsman, the
specific sub-clause said to bar the claim; and (f) reiterated the same
unreasoned ground across the claim, grievance and post-Award stages,
thereby causing the Complainant financial loss and prolonged mental
agony across more than a year and three successive fora. Each of these,
and certainly their cumulative effect, amounts to deficiency in service
under Section 2(11) and to an unfair trade practice under Section 2(47)
of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
